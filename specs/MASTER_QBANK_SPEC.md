# MCCQE QUESTION BANK — MASTER CONTENT SPECIFICATION

Version: 1.0
Primary study reference: Toronto Notes 2025, 41st Edition
Target: ~6,000 original questions
Disciplines:
- Medicine
- Pediatrics
- Obstetrics & Gynecology
- Surgery
- Psychiatry
- PHELO

This document is the authoritative specification for all question-generation,
verification, audit, and export tasks.

Do not override these rules in an individual batch unless explicitly instructed.

======================================================================
1. PURPOSE
======================================================================

Create a comprehensive ORIGINAL practice question bank for the current
Medical Council of Canada Qualifying Examination (MCCQE).

The intended learner is:

- a Canadian medical student approaching graduation, OR
- an international medical graduate preparing for the MCCQE.

The goal is to reproduce:

- scope
- clinical reasoning level
- question construction
- decision-making style
- breadth
- Canadian context

of the current MCCQE.

DO NOT attempt to reproduce protected MCC examination content.

This resource must contain only original questions.

======================================================================
2. COPYRIGHT / EXAM SECURITY
======================================================================

NEVER:

- reproduce actual MCCQE questions;
- use recalled/leaked MCCQE questions;
- reconstruct protected MCC items;
- closely paraphrase official paid MCC questions;
- copy commercial Qbank questions;
- reproduce Toronto Notes questions;
- reproduce long passages from Toronto Notes;
- reproduce Toronto Notes tables, diagrams, mnemonics or explanatory prose.

Public MCC sample questions may be analyzed ONLY to calibrate:

- stem construction;
- vignette length;
- information density;
- lead-in style;
- difficulty;
- distractor quality;
- expected reasoning.

Do not reuse their cases, answer choices, distinctive facts or wording.

Toronto Notes may be used as a legally provided study reference.

For Toronto Notes record only:

- edition;
- chapter;
- chapter code;
- section/subsection;
- Toronto Notes page range;
- physical PDF page range.

Do not store copied Toronto Notes prose inside the Qbank.

======================================================================
3. SOURCE ROLES — KEEP THESE SEPARATE
======================================================================

There are THREE distinct kinds of sources.

------------------------------
A. STUDY ORGANIZATION SOURCE
------------------------------

Toronto Notes 2025.

Purpose:

- determine chapter order;
- determine subtopic order;
- identify breadth of clinical topics;
- provide "Study in Toronto Notes" anchors;
- identify areas the learner should review.

Toronto Notes DOES NOT automatically determine the current correct answer.

------------------------------
B. EXAM SCOPE SOURCE
------------------------------

Current Medical Council of Canada Examination Objectives.

Purpose:

- determine expected graduate-level competency;
- identify relevant presentations;
- determine whether a topic belongs at MCCQE level;
- map questions to MCC Objectives.

The current MCCQE Blueprint and Study Smarter guide should also be consulted.

------------------------------
C. CLINICAL ANSWER SOURCE
------------------------------

Current authoritative medical guidance.

This determines whether an answer is medically correct TODAY.

The current guideline takes priority over Toronto Notes 2025 when guidance
has changed.

======================================================================
4. CLINICAL SOURCE HIERARCHY
======================================================================

Prefer sources in approximately this order.

TIER 1 — CANADIAN NATIONAL / EXAM

- Medical Council of Canada
- Government of Canada
- Health Canada
- Public Health Agency of Canada

TIER 2 — CANADIAN SPECIALTY GUIDANCE

Examples include:

- Canadian Paediatric Society
- Society of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists of Canada
- Canadian Cardiovascular Society
- Diabetes Canada
- Hypertension Canada
- Canadian Thoracic Society
- Canadian Association of Gastroenterology
- Thrombosis Canada
- CANMAT
- Canadian Rheumatology Association
- Canadian Neurological organizations
- Canadian Urological Association
- Choosing Wisely Canada
- other authoritative Canadian specialty societies

TIER 3 — PROVINCIAL / JURISDICTIONAL

Use when jurisdiction matters:

- provincial Colleges of Physicians and Surgeons;
- provincial public-health authorities;
- provincial screening programs;
- provincial/federal statutes;
- CMPA;
- CMA;
- authoritative provincial health agencies.

TIER 4 — INTERNATIONAL / PEER REVIEWED

Use when no appropriate current Canadian source exists:

- high-quality specialty guidelines;
- systematic reviews;
- major peer-reviewed consensus guidance.

When Canadian practice differs from international guidance,
Canadian guidance wins for an MCCQE-oriented question.

======================================================================
5. SOURCE-FIRST GENERATION
======================================================================

NEVER use this workflow:

LLM remembers fact
→ writes question
→ searches for citation afterward.

REQUIRED workflow:

Toronto Notes section
→ MCC Objective
→ authoritative current source
→ verified clinical decision
→ original question
→ rationale
→ independent verification.

Before an item is created, establish:

1. the Toronto Notes study section;
2. the relevant MCC Objective;
3. the exact clinical decision being tested;
4. an authoritative source supporting that decision.

If these cannot be established, DO NOT create the question.

======================================================================
6. CURRENT MCCQE STYLE
======================================================================

At the beginning of the project and periodically thereafter,
verify the CURRENT MCCQE format from mcc.ca.

Use current public MCC item-writing guidance.

Unless current official MCC documentation has changed, construct questions as:

- single-best-answer;
- clinical stem;
- clear lead-in;
- FIVE options;
- one keyed response;
- four plausible distractors.

Use five options by default.

Question difficulty must arise from medical reasoning, NOT misleading wording.

======================================================================
7. WHAT THE QUESTIONS SHOULD TEST
======================================================================

Emphasize:

- most likely diagnosis;
- differential diagnosis;
- discriminating clinical finding;
- initial investigation;
- best next investigation;
- interpretation of laboratory/imaging findings;
- immediate stabilization;
- most appropriate next management step;
- first-line management;
- contraindications;
- complication recognition;
- prevention;
- screening;
- follow-up;
- communication;
- professional behaviour;
- ethical/legal decision making.

Approximately align the total bank with MCC physician activities:

- Assessment / Diagnosis: ~45%
- Management: ~35%
- Communication: ~10%
- Professional Behaviours: ~10%

And dimensions of care:

- Health Promotion / Illness Prevention: ~20%
- Acute: ~35%
- Chronic: ~30%
- Psychosocial: ~15%

These percentages apply most strongly across the COMPLETE bank/exam,
not necessarily rigidly inside every narrow subtopic.

======================================================================
8. DEPTH
======================================================================

Target:

Canadian graduating medical student entering supervised practice.

NOT:

specialty board examination.

Prioritize knowledge that changes clinical action.

High-value details include:

- diagnostic criteria;
- meaningful thresholds;
- red flags;
- initial investigations;
- treatment sequence;
- contraindications;
- emergency management;
- common drug adverse effects;
- screening recommendations;
- vaccination;
- follow-up;
- complications.

Usually LOW VALUE:

- obscure molecular pathways;
- rare mutations;
- detailed surgical technique;
- complex specialist staging systems;
- exact chemotherapy regimens;
- rare histopathological trivia;
- uncommon eponyms;
- highly specialized procedural details.

======================================================================
9. DIFFICULTY
======================================================================

Target approximately:

20% straightforward
60% moderate
20% difficult

DIFFICULT DOES NOT MEAN OBSCURE.

A difficult item should be difficult because, for example:

- two diagnoses overlap clinically;
- one finding distinguishes them;
- several actions may eventually be required but only one happens FIRST;
- the learner must interpret investigations;
- comorbidity changes the management;
- pregnancy/age/renal function/medications change the answer;
- Canadian screening or public-health guidance matters;
- the learner must recognize an unstable patient.

Never manufacture difficulty through:

- misleading wording;
- intentional omission of necessary facts;
- trivia;
- double negatives;
- unusual terminology;
- overly long irrelevant stems.

======================================================================
10. STEM CONSTRUCTION
======================================================================

Prefer realistic clinical vignettes.

Include only information that:

- establishes context;
- helps form the differential;
- affects the diagnosis;
- affects management;
- rules alternatives in/out.

Possible stem components:

- age;
- sex where clinically relevant;
- pregnancy status;
- presenting complaint;
- time course;
- relevant history;
- medication history;
- vital signs;
- focused examination;
- investigations.

Do not overload every question with unnecessary details.

Use Canadian/SI units.

When interpretation of laboratory data requires a reference range,
provide the appropriate normal range.

Use patient-first, respectful language.

Avoid unnecessary demographic details when they do not affect clinical reasoning.

======================================================================
11. LEAD-IN CONSTRUCTION
======================================================================

The lead-in must ask ONE clear question.

Preferred forms include:

- Which one of the following is the most likely diagnosis?
- Which one of the following is the most appropriate next step?
- Which one of the following investigations should be obtained next?
- Which one of the following is the most appropriate initial treatment?
- Which one of the following findings best explains this presentation?
- Which one of the following is the most appropriate preventive intervention?

Avoid vague questions such as:

- What should you do?
- Which statement is true?

unless context makes the task unequivocal.

Avoid EXCEPT/NOT questions whenever possible.

======================================================================
12. OPTIONS
======================================================================

Default: FIVE options.

All options should belong to the same logical category.

If the lead-in asks for treatment:
all options should be treatments.

If diagnosis:
all should be diagnoses.

Distractors must:

- be plausible;
- reflect genuine misconceptions;
- be inferior to the keyed answer;
- have similar length/structure;
- not contain obvious grammatical clues;
- not be absurd;
- not be mutually incompatible with the stem in an obvious way.

Never use:

- All of the above
- None of the above

unless current MCC guidance explicitly changes.

Avoid answer-position patterns.

======================================================================
13. QUESTION DIFFICULTY THROUGH SEQUENCING
======================================================================

Strong MCCQE-style questions often distinguish:

- stabilization vs definitive treatment;
- initial test vs confirmatory test;
- urgent action vs later management;
- screening vs diagnostic testing;
- outpatient vs emergency management;
- referral vs immediate intervention.

If several options are eventually appropriate,
the question must clearly specify what point in the sequence is being tested.

======================================================================
14. RATIONALES
======================================================================

EVERY published question must have an educational explanation.

Required structure:

A. CORRECT ANSWER
- option letter;
- option text.

B. ANSWER SUMMARY
- concise 1–3 sentence explanation.

C. CLINICAL REASONING
- detailed explanation;
- usually ~150–350 words;
- longer only when complexity requires it.

D. KEY CLUES
- 2–6 bullet-sized concepts;
- explain what each clue contributes.

E. WHY THE CORRECT OPTION IS BEST
- explicitly connect evidence → clinical decision.

F. WHY EACH DISTRACTOR IS LESS APPROPRIATE
- every distractor;
- generally 1–3 substantive sentences;
- explain when the distractor WOULD be appropriate when educationally useful.

G. WHAT HAPPENS NEXT
- optional but encouraged for sequential management questions.

H. CLINICAL PEARL
- one concise takeaway.

I. WHAT THIS QUESTION TESTS
- MCC Objective;
- physician activity;
- dimension of care.

Do not produce shallow explanations such as:

"A is wrong because B is correct."

======================================================================
15. REFERENCES
======================================================================

Every question requires at least ONE authoritative clinical reference.

Prefer 1–3.

For each reference capture:

{
  "reference_id": "...",
  "title": "...",
  "organization": "...",
  "url": "...",
  "publication_or_update_date": "...",
  "accessed_date": "...",
  "source_tier": 1,
  "supports": [
    {
      "claim": "...",
      "locator": "section/table/recommendation/etc."
    }
  ]
}

A citation is NOT verified merely because the URL exists.

The source must actually support the claim.

Never:

- invent an article;
- invent a guideline;
- invent a DOI;
- invent a URL;
- attach an unrelated legitimate guideline to a claim.

If access to the source is insufficient to establish support:
do not publish the question as verified.

======================================================================
16. HIGH-RISK FACTS
======================================================================

Apply EXTRA verification to:

- medication doses;
- medication contraindications;
- pregnancy medication recommendations;
- pediatric doses;
- age cutoffs;
- gestational ages;
- screening eligibility;
- screening intervals;
- vaccine schedules;
- diagnostic numerical criteria;
- blood-pressure thresholds;
- laboratory cutoffs;
- treatment intervals;
- anticoagulation decisions;
- public-health isolation periods;
- mandatory reporting;
- legal requirements;
- province-specific medical law.

These should preferably have TWO corroborating authoritative sources
when practical.

======================================================================
17. CANADIAN LAW
======================================================================

Never assume a provincial rule is Canada-wide.

For legal questions classify the rule as:

- federal;
- nationwide professional principle;
- provincial/territorial;
- institution-dependent.

If provincial:

- explicitly state province when required;
- verify the current provincial rule.

If jurisdiction affects the answer but is not stated:
rewrite or reject the question.

======================================================================
19. TORONTO NOTES VS CURRENT GUIDANCE
======================================================================

If current authoritative guidance differs from Toronto Notes 2025:

- current guidance wins;
- do not create ambiguity;
- tag:

"guideline_updated_since_tn2025": true

- record the current source;
- do not criticize or reproduce the outdated wording;
- optionally include a short learner note that current guidance was used.

======================================================================
24. BLIND VERIFICATION
======================================================================

A different verification pass must solve the completed question WITHOUT seeing:

- intended correct answer;
- rationale;
- generator reasoning.

Provide verifier only:

- stem;
- lead-in;
- options;
- Toronto Notes mapping;
- MCC mapping;
- permitted sources/references.

The verifier independently chooses the best answer.

Then compare programmatically.

Generator answer != verifier answer:

QUARANTINE.

Do NOT silently change the key.

======================================================================
25. VERIFICATION GATES
======================================================================

A question cannot become production-ready unless:

[ ] five options
[ ] exactly one intended best answer
[ ] blind verifier independently selects same answer
[ ] verifier confidence acceptable
[ ] no second defensible answer
[ ] current clinical source supports key
[ ] relevant distractors are definitely inferior
[ ] required numerical facts verified
[ ] Canadian context correct
[ ] Toronto Notes mapping valid
[ ] MCC Objective mapping valid
[ ] rationale medically consistent
[ ] every distractor explained
[ ] not an exact duplicate
[ ] not a strong semantic duplicate
[ ] no copyrighted-question similarity detected
[ ] appropriate graduate-level depth

======================================================================
26. FAIL CLOSED
======================================================================

When uncertain:

REJECT rather than publish.

Examples:

- unable to verify source;
- two reasonable answers;
- unclear province;
- guideline conflict;
- missing clinical information;
- questionable numerical cutoff;
- outdated recommendation;
- specialist-level ambiguity.

A smaller reliable bank is better than a larger unreliable bank.

======================================================================
27. HUMAN REVIEW
======================================================================

Allowed statuses:

- draft
- source-verified
- blind-check-passed
- QA-passed
- human-reviewed

NEVER label an item:

- physician reviewed;
- doctor approved;
- clinically validated;

unless an actual qualified human reviewer reviewed that specific item.

For eventual public deployment, prioritize human review of:

- PHELO/legal;
- pregnancy treatment;
- pediatrics;
- vaccination;
- screening;
- anticoagulation;
- medication dosing;
- questions flagged as difficult/controversial;
- random QA samples from every chapter.

======================================================================
28. DUPLICATE CONTROL
======================================================================

Check:

- exact normalized text duplicates;
- near-identical stems;
- same vignette with minor demographic changes;
- same clinical decision repeatedly tested;
- repeated distractor sets;
- repeated numbers/labs creating memorization cues.

Semantic similarity should flag candidate duplicates for review.

Do not simply delete automatically when similarity is uncertain.

======================================================================
29. ANSWER DISTRIBUTION
======================================================================

Across meaningful samples, A/B/C/D/E should be reasonably balanced.

Never intentionally force an exact pattern.

Reject detectable sequences such as:

A B C D E A B C D E

Randomize after question construction where possible,
then revalidate answer/options.

31. PUBLICATION RULE
======================================================================

Only:

verification.final_status = "QA-passed"

or

verification.final_status = "human-reviewed"

may enter the production Qbank.

Candidate, rejected, retired and needs-review items must never be included
in the student-facing production manifest.

======================================================================
32. ACCURACY OVER QUANTITY
======================================================================

The target is approximately 1,000 questions per discipline.

This is a target, not permission to publish questionable content.

If only 963 items pass the required validation:
publish 963 and report the gap.

Do not invent weak questions merely to reach exactly 1,000.
